\section{Theoretical Analysis: RSP as Attention-Mediated Exploration}\label{sec:why_rsp_works}

We isolate a transformer-level mechanism explaining the empirical regularities of Section~\ref{sec:experimental_analysis}: RSP affects the hidden state before the output softmax, its influence is controlled by the attention mass assigned to the random tokens, and this influence is structurally diluted as real-token evidence accumulates. The argument is built from standard self-attention identities; in particular, none of the formal results in Sections~\ref{sec:energy_landscape}--\ref{sec:multipath} assume $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{V}$, weight-tied layers, or the existence of a global energy.

% ================================================================
\subsection{What RSP Is: Scale-Matched Isotropic Perturbation}\label{sec:rsp_design}
% ================================================================

\begin{definition}[Random Soft Prompt]\label{def:rsp}
Let $\mathbf{W}_E\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times d}$ be the pretrained input-embedding table, with $\mu_E$ and $\sigma_E$ the entrywise scalar mean and standard deviation defined in Section~\ref{sec:rsp_definition}. An \emph{RSP} of length $k$ is a sequence $(h_1,\dots,h_k)$ with $h_j = \mu_E\mathbf{1}_d + \sigma_E\,\xi_j$ and $\xi_j\stackrel{\text{iid}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_d)$. The centered prompt $\bar h_j := h_j-\mu_E\mathbf{1}_d$ satisfies $\bar h_j\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_E^2\mathbf{I}_d)$. Whenever we analyze repeated-sampling protocols such as Pass@$N$, we additionally assume the prompt draws and any decoding randomness used across compared trials are independent.
\end{definition}

This is not a model of the empirical embedding distribution, which is generally anisotropic \citep{mu2018allbutthetop, ethayarajh2019contextual}. RSP deliberately discards directional geometry and replaces the row-covariance of $\mathbf{W}_E$ by the isotropic $\sigma_E^2\mathbf{I}_d$: $\sigma_E^2$ borrows only the \emph{scale}, so that the perturbation operates in the magnitude regime the model expects, while the identity covariance \emph{deliberately} discards direction. The reason is that the branch-sensitive direction at a decoding state depends on prompt, layer, position, and the specific token pair compared; a generic exploration perturbation should not privilege a small set of embedding directions.

% ================================================================
\subsection{Why Randomness Is Necessary for Exploration}\label{sec:why_random_prompt}
% ================================================================

\emph{RSP is not random because we believe the embedding table is Gaussian; it is random because exploration requires resampling, centering, and direction-agnostic coverage.} A fixed continuous prompt can steer a model, but reusing the same vector across rollouts produces the same perturbation direction every time, so it cannot generate independent alternatives for repeated sampling. Discrete random tokens are also inadequate, because each vocabulary token carries a lexical and semantic direction inherited from pretraining: discrete sampling injects uncontrolled semantic priors. RSP avoids both by using continuous vectors that are centered and isotropic rather than tied to any specific embedding direction. We formalize the choice through two properties.

\begin{lemma}[Semantic neutrality]\label{lem:semantic_neutrality}
Let $\bar h\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2\mathbf{I}_d)$ and $\hat h := \bar h/\|\bar h\|$. For any fixed unit vectors $u_1,\dots,u_M\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ and $\delta\in(0,1)$,
\[
\Pr\!\left[\max_{r\in[M]}|\hat h^\top u_r| \le \sqrt{\tfrac{2\log(2M/\delta)}{d-1}}\right]
\;\ge\;1-\delta.
\]
\end{lemma}
A token embedding does not have this property: any fixed direction $u=e_s/\|e_s\|$ gives $|\hat e_s^\top u|=1$. Lemma~\ref{lem:semantic_neutrality} therefore says, precisely, that an isotropic random direction is nearly orthogonal to any fixed finite collection of semantic anchors with high probability; it does \emph{not} claim that a single random vector is free of every incidental correlation.

\begin{theorem}[Direction-agnostic coverage]\label{thm:minimax_directional_coverage}
Let $D$ be any zero-mean perturbation distribution on $\mathbb{R}^d$ with covariance $\Sigma_D$ and trace budget $\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma_D)\le \rho^2$. Define the worst directional variance $\mathcal{E}(D) := \min_{\|b\|=1} \mathrm{Var}_{h\sim D}(b^\top h)$. Then
\[
\mathcal{E}(D) \;=\; \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_D) \;\le\; \frac{\rho^2}{d},
\]
with equality if and only if $\Sigma_D = (\rho^2/d)\mathbf{I}_d$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:minimax_directional_coverage} is a minimax statement: among all zero-mean perturbations with the same total variance budget, isotropic covariance uniquely maximizes the least-covered direction. Together with Lemma~\ref{lem:semantic_neutrality} this rules out the most natural alternatives: a fixed prompt has $\Sigma_D=0$ and produces no exploration; PCA-aligned noise is anisotropic and underexplores the orthogonal complement; vocabulary-token sampling is both anisotropic and semantically committing. The same theorem also rules out the apparently natural alternative of \emph{matching} the empirical embedding covariance.

\begin{corollary}[Embedding-matched noise is suboptimal]\label{cor:embedding_matched_suboptimal}
Let $\Sigma_E$ be the row-covariance of $\mathbf{W}_E$ around the same scalar-broadcast center $\mu_E\mathbf{1}_d$ used in Definition~\ref{def:rsp}, with $\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma_E)=d\sigma_E^2$. Compare two zero-mean perturbations on $\mathbb{R}^d$ with the common trace budget $\rho^2 := d\sigma_E^2$: the isotropic RSP law $D_{\mathrm{iso}}$ with covariance $\sigma_E^2\mathbf{I}_d$, and the embedding-matched law $D_{\mathrm{emb}}$ with covariance $\Sigma_E$. Then $\mathcal{E}(D_{\mathrm{iso}}) = \sigma_E^2 \ge \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_E) = \mathcal{E}(D_{\mathrm{emb}})$, with strict inequality whenever $\Sigma_E$ is non-isotropic.
\end{corollary}

The corollary requires no separate proof beyond Theorem~\ref{thm:minimax_directional_coverage}. To the extent that pretrained embedding tables inherit the documented anisotropy of word and contextual representations \citep{mu2018allbutthetop, ethayarajh2019contextual}, $\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_E)$ is strictly smaller than $\sigma_E^2$, so an embedding-matched alternative leaves at least one direction less explored than the isotropic comparator. Discarding the directional geometry of $\mathbf{W}_E$ while keeping its scale is therefore not a simplification but a deliberately better choice for direction-agnostic exploration.

\subsection{Exact Transformer-Level Mechanism}\label{sec:energy_landscape}
% ================================================================

We now show that the effect of RSP in a standard transformer is controlled by the attention mass assigned to the random tokens. None of the statements in this subsection assume $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{V}$, weight-tied layers, or the existence of a global energy function; they follow from standard self-attention identities. (A complementary interpretation as iterative retrieval on a Hopfield-style energy landscape \citep{ramsauer2021hopfield} is given in Appendix~\ref{app:hopfield_descent}; the formal results below do not depend on that view.)

\paragraph{Attention decomposition.}
Consider a self-attention layer $\ell$ with $n$ real tokens and $k$ RSP tokens. Let $\alpha_{ij}^{(\ell)}$ denote the attention weight from query position $i$ to token $j$. Define the random-token attention mass
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wr}
w_{r,i}^{(\ell)} \;=\; \sum_{j=1}^{k} \alpha_{i,n+j}^{(\ell)},
\end{equation}
and write $\mathbf{v}_j^{(\ell)}$ and $\mathbf{v}_{r_j}^{(\ell)}$ for the value vectors of the $j$-th real and the $j$-th RSP token, respectively. The attention output decomposes \emph{exactly} as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decomp}
\mathbf{x}_i^{(\ell+1)}
\;=\;
(1-w_{r,i}^{(\ell)})\,\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i^{(\ell+1)}
\;+\;
\boldsymbol{\eta}_i^{(\ell)},
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i^{(\ell+1)} = \sum_{j=1}^n \tfrac{\alpha_{ij}^{(\ell)}}{1-w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}}\mathbf{v}_j^{(\ell)}$ is the renormalized attention output over the real tokens alone and $\boldsymbol{\eta}_i^{(\ell)} = \sum_{j=1}^k \alpha_{i,n+j}^{(\ell)}\mathbf{v}_{r_j}^{(\ell)}$ is the RSP contribution. By the triangle inequality, $\|\boldsymbol{\eta}_i^{(\ell)}\| \le w_{r,i}^{(\ell)} \max_{j\in[k]}\|\mathbf{v}_{r_j}^{(\ell)}\|$. Hence the same scalar $w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}$ controls both how much real-token attention is displaced and how large the random contribution can be. Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomp} is an algebraic identity, not an approximation; the proof and the deterministic bound on $\boldsymbol{\eta}_i^{(\ell)}$ are in Appendix~\ref{app:rsp_noise}.

\paragraph{Sequence-wise dilution.}
With $\Delta_i^{(\ell)} := \min_{j\in[n]}\mathbf{q}_i^\top\mathbf{k}_j - \max_{j'\in[k]}\mathbf{q}_i^\top\mathbf{k}_{r_{j'}}$ the real-vs-random pre-scaling logit gap, the random-token mass satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decay_main}
w_{r,i}^{(\ell)} \;\le\; \frac{k}{k + n\,\exp(\Delta_i^{(\ell)}/\sqrt d)}.
\end{equation}
For fixed $k$ and $\Delta_i^{(\ell)}$ the right-hand side is strictly decreasing in $n$, so autoregressive cache growth alone imposes a monotone decrease on the largest random-token attention compatible with that gap. This is a structural dilution effect, not monotone decay of the realized $w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}$ on every path: $\Delta_i^{(\ell)}$ itself evolves with state, and layer-wise decay (deeper layers widen $\Delta_i^{(\ell)}$ as real-token retrieval sharpens) is a model-side hypothesis verified empirically by Figure~\ref{fig:attention_mass}.

\paragraph{Block-level propagation.}
A full transformer block adds residual connections, normalization, and a feed-forward sublayer. Under a common local Lipschitz constant for the full block map (covering the attention sublayer, LayerNorm, and the FFN) on a ball containing both the perturbed and unperturbed trajectories---which holds in practice because each component is locally Lipschitz on bounded sets \citep{xiong2020layer, kim2021lipschitz}---Appendix~\ref{app:ffn_lipschitz} shows that the per-block perturbation amplitude scales linearly with $w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}$, so iterating across a finite depth gives a finite cumulative bound. Combined with Eq.~\eqref{eq:decay_main}, this explains why the perturbation enters strongly at early blocks and shrinks as evidence accumulates, without requiring a persistent perturbation throughout the reasoning chain.

\paragraph{Empirical verification.}\label{sec:attention_mass}
We test these model-dependent steps on Qwen2.5-Math-7B under suffix injection. If $w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}$ acts as an annealed exploration coefficient, then the realized attention to RSP tokens should decay across reasoning positions and layers, while attention to question tokens should not exhibit the same monotone pattern because they remain task-relevant. Figure~\ref{fig:attention_mass} shows this contrast.

\subsection{Output Support Expansion Beyond Temperature}\label{sec:output_shift}
% ================================================================

Temperature sampling is monotone, $p_i^{(\tau)}\propto p_i^{1/\tau}$, so $p_i>p_j\iff p_i^{(\tau)}>p_j^{(\tau)}$ for any $\tau>0$: it cannot change token rankings at a fixed decoding state. RSP acts before the output softmax. Throughout this subsection $\bar h\in\mathbb{R}^d$ denotes one centered prompt vector from Definition~\ref{def:rsp}, treated as a per-token surrogate; the multi-token compounding through softmax-weighted attention is handled empirically by Section~\ref{sec:diversity}. The two propositions below are stated for the local affine surrogate $z_i^{\mathrm{lin}}(\bar h):= z_i + a_i^\top\bar h$ with $a_i := \nabla_{\bar h} z_i(0)$, valid for small $\|\bar h\|$ (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}); they identify the structure of pre-softmax rank flips under that surrogate, while the multi-token, fully nonlinear effect is reported empirically in Section~\ref{sec:diversity}.

\begin{proposition}[Stochastic ranking under the local affine surrogate]\label{prop:stochastic_ranking}
If there exists a token pair $(i,j)$ with $a_i\neq a_j$ and the centered prompt law assigns positive probability to every nonempty open subset of $\mathbb{R}^d$, then $0<\Pr_{\bar h}(z_i^{\mathrm{lin}}(\bar h)>z_j^{\mathrm{lin}}(\bar h))<1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Top-$K$ entry under the local affine surrogate]\label{prop:support_expansion}
Let $J_K(s_t)$ be the baseline top-$K$ token set at decoding state $s_t$ and $i\notin J_K(s_t)$. Define the surrogate entry region
\[
\mathcal{G}_{i,K}
:=
\big\{\bar h\in\mathbb{R}^d:\ \#\{j\neq i: z_j^{\mathrm{lin}}(\bar h)>z_i^{\mathrm{lin}}(\bar h)\}\le K-1\big\}.
\]
A sufficient condition for $\mathcal{G}_{i,K}$ to contain a nonempty open set is the existence of $\bar h_0\in\mathbb{R}^d$ at which token $i$ outranks all but at most $K-1$ other tokens \emph{with a strict margin} (no tie); the affine surrogate is continuous, so an open ball around such $\bar h_0$ lies in $\mathcal{G}_{i,K}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). If this condition holds and the centered prompt law assigns positive probability to every nonempty open subset of $\mathbb{R}^d$, then $\Pr_{\bar h}(\bar h\in\mathcal{G}_{i,K})>0$.
\end{proposition}


Proposition~\ref{prop:support_expansion} is intentionally conditional: it does not claim every excluded token admits an entry region. The complementary case in which token $i$ is dominated by the baseline top-$K$ in the affine surrogate is exactly the case in which the proposition makes no claim. Temperature scaling reaches no $\mathcal{G}_{i,K}$ at all because it never changes token order; the mechanism here is branch opening, not probability flattening.

\subsection{From Independent Perturbations to Pass@$N$}\label{sec:multipath}
% ================================================================

Let $\mu_i := \Pr_{\bar h}(\bar h\in\mathcal{G}_{i,K}) > 0$.

\begin{corollary}[Sample complexity of admission]\label{cor:sample_complexity}
Within $N$ independent fresh RSP draws, $\Pr(\text{at least one admits } i \text{ into top-}K) = 1-(1-\mu_i)^N \ge 1-\exp(-N\mu_i)$.
\end{corollary}

Lifting this to task accuracy requires an additional task-dependent assumption.

\begin{proposition}[Exploration-meets-multi-path accuracy gain]\label{prop:multi_path}
Fix a problem $x$. Run the deterministic baseline once and draw $N$ fresh independent RSPs, decoding once per sample. Let $\mathcal{T}^\star(x)$ be the nonempty set of correct trajectories and $p_{\mathrm{base}}(x)\in\{0,1\}$ the baseline correctness indicator. Assume \emph{(M1)} for each $n$, the marginal probability that the $n$-th RSP run produces a trajectory in $\mathcal{T}^\star(x)$ is at least $p_{\min}(x)>0$, and \emph{(M2)} the $N$ RSP runs are mutually independent. Then $\Pr\!\big(\mathrm{Pass@}(N{+}1)\text{ correct on }x\big) \ge \max\!\big\{p_{\mathrm{base}}(x),\,1-(1-p_{\min}(x))^N\big\} \ge 1-e^{-Np_{\min}(x)}$.
\end{proposition}

(M1) is task-side; Section~\ref{sec:diversity} reports the empirical signature, with RSP-induced trajectories including both improvements and regressions and net gain under repeated sampling.
