\section{Theoretical Analysis: RSP as Attention-Mediated Exploration}\label{sec:why_rsp_works}

The empirical regularities of Section~\ref{sec:experimental_analysis}---raised early-stage entropy, later stabilization, and Pass@$N$ gains under repeated decoding---admit a single transformer-level explanation. RSP perturbs the hidden state before the output softmax through one scalar quantity, the random-token attention mass, which autoregressive cache growth structurally dilutes. The randomness in RSP is not a model of the embedding table: it is what lets the same prompt produce \emph{independent} perturbations across rollouts, and exploration requires nothing less than independent perturbations. The argument that follows uses only standard self-attention identities, and in particular assumes neither $\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{V}$, weight tying, nor a global energy function.

% ================================================================
\subsection{Scale-Matched Isotropic Perturbation}\label{sec:rsp_design}
% ================================================================

\begin{definition}[Random Soft Prompt]\label{def:rsp}
Let $\mathbf{W}_E\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times d}$ be the pretrained input-embedding table with entrywise scalar mean $\mu_E$ and standard deviation $\sigma_E$. An RSP of length $k$ is a sequence $(h_1,\dots,h_k)$ with $h_j = \mu_E\mathbf{1}_d + \sigma_E\,\xi_j$ and $\xi_j\stackrel{\text{iid}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_d)$. The centered prompt $\bar h_j := h_j-\mu_E\mathbf{1}_d$ then satisfies $\bar h_j\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_E^2\mathbf{I}_d)$. For repeated-sampling protocols (Pass@$N$), prompt draws and any decoding randomness across compared trials are assumed independent.
\end{definition}

The empirical embedding distribution is generally anisotropic \citep{mu2018allbutthetop, ethayarajh2019contextual}, and RSP departs from it on purpose. Borrowing $\sigma_E$ keeps the perturbation in the magnitude regime the model expects, while the identity covariance discards directional structure deliberately. The branch-sensitive direction at any given decoding state depends on prompt, layer, position, and the specific token pair compared, so a generic exploration perturbation should not commit in advance to a small set of embedding directions.

% ================================================================
\subsection{Why Randomness Is Necessary}\label{sec:why_random_prompt}
% ================================================================

Exploration requires variability across trials, and that variability cannot be supplied by a fixed perturbation. A trained continuous prompt can steer a model, but reusing the same vector across rollouts produces the same perturbation direction every time and so cannot generate independent alternatives. Discrete random tokens fail differently: each vocabulary token carries a lexical and semantic direction inherited from pretraining, so token-level sampling injects uncontrolled semantic priors. RSP avoids both failure modes by being continuous, centered, and isotropic, and the next two results pin down what each of those properties buys.

\begin{lemma}[Semantic neutrality]\label{lem:semantic_neutrality}
Let $\bar h\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2\mathbf{I}_d)$ and $\hat h := \bar h/\|\bar h\|$. For any fixed unit vectors $u_1,\dots,u_M\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ and $\delta\in(0,1)$,
\[
\Pr\!\left[\max_{r\in[M]}|\hat h^\top u_r| \;\le\; \sqrt{\tfrac{2\log(2M/\delta)}{d-1}}\right]\;\ge\;1-\delta.
\]
\end{lemma}

A token embedding fails this property: any fixed direction $u=e_s/\|e_s\|$ gives $|\hat e_s^\top u|=1$. Lemma~\ref{lem:semantic_neutrality} formalizes only the weaker, sufficient claim that an isotropic random direction is nearly orthogonal to any fixed finite collection of semantic anchors with high probability; it does not assert that a single random vector is free of every incidental correlation.

\begin{theorem}[Direction-agnostic coverage]\label{thm:minimax_directional_coverage}
Let $D$ be any zero-mean perturbation on $\mathbb{R}^d$ with covariance $\Sigma_D$ and trace budget $\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma_D)\le \rho^2$. Then $\min_{\|b\|=1}\mathrm{Var}_{h\sim D}(b^\top h) = \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_D) \le \rho^2/d$, with equality if and only if $\Sigma_D = (\rho^2/d)\mathbf{I}_d$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:minimax_directional_coverage} is a minimax statement: among zero-mean perturbations sharing a total-variance budget, isotropic covariance uniquely maximizes the least-explored direction. Together with Lemma~\ref{lem:semantic_neutrality} this rules out the natural alternatives at once. A fixed prompt has $\Sigma_D=0$ and provides no exploration; PCA-aligned noise is anisotropic and underexplores the orthogonal complement; vocabulary-token sampling is anisotropic \emph{and} semantically committing. The same theorem also rules out the apparently natural alternative of matching the empirical embedding covariance: writing $\Sigma_E$ for the row-covariance of $\mathbf{W}_E$ around the same scalar center $\mu_E\mathbf{1}_d$ with $\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma_E)=d\sigma_E^2$, the embedding-matched law has $\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_E)<\sigma_E^2$ whenever $\Sigma_E$ is non-isotropic, so it leaves at least one direction strictly less explored than the isotropic comparator. Discarding directional geometry while keeping scale is therefore a deliberate optimization, not a simplification.

\subsection{Exact Transformer-Level Mechanism}\label{sec:energy_landscape}
% ================================================================

The effect of RSP in a standard transformer is controlled by the attention mass assigned to the random tokens. Consider self-attention layer $\ell$ with $n$ real and $k$ RSP tokens, attention weights $\alpha_{ij}^{(\ell)}$, and value vectors $\mathbf{v}_j^{(\ell)},\mathbf{v}_{r_j}^{(\ell)}$ for real and RSP tokens. Let
\[
w_{r,i}^{(\ell)} \;=\; \sum_{j=1}^{k} \alpha_{i,n+j}^{(\ell)}
\]
denote the random-token attention mass. The attention output then decomposes \emph{exactly} as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decomp}
\mathbf{x}_i^{(\ell+1)}
\;=\;
(1-w_{r,i}^{(\ell)})\,\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i^{(\ell+1)}
\;+\;
\boldsymbol{\eta}_i^{(\ell)},
\qquad
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i^{(\ell+1)} \;=\; \sum_{j=1}^n \tfrac{\alpha_{ij}^{(\ell)}}{1-w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}}\,\mathbf{v}_j^{(\ell)},
\end{equation}
with random contribution $\boldsymbol{\eta}_i^{(\ell)} = \sum_{j=1}^k \alpha_{i,n+j}^{(\ell)}\mathbf{v}_{r_j}^{(\ell)}$ obeying $\|\boldsymbol{\eta}_i^{(\ell)}\| \le w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}\,\max_{j}\|\mathbf{v}_{r_j}^{(\ell)}\|$. The same scalar $w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}$ thus controls both how much real-token signal is displaced and how large the random contribution can be; Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomp} is an algebraic identity rather than an approximation, and the deterministic norm bound is recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:rsp_noise}. (A complementary interpretation as iterative retrieval on a Hopfield-style energy landscape \citep{ramsauer2021hopfield} is offered in Appendix~\ref{app:hopfield_descent}; the formal results in this section do not depend on it.)

The dilution structure follows directly. With $\Delta_i^{(\ell)} := \min_{j\in[n]}\mathbf{q}_i^\top\mathbf{k}_j - \max_{j'\in[k]}\mathbf{q}_i^\top\mathbf{k}_{r_{j'}}$ the real-vs-random pre-scaling logit gap, scaled dot-product attention gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decay_main}
w_{r,i}^{(\ell)} \;\le\; \frac{k}{k + n\,\exp(\Delta_i^{(\ell)}/\sqrt d)},
\end{equation}
which is strictly decreasing in $n$ at fixed $k$ and $\Delta_i^{(\ell)}$. Autoregressive cache growth alone therefore imposes a monotone decrease on the largest random-token attention compatible with the gap; this is structural dilution of an upper bound, not monotone decay of every realized $w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}$, since $\Delta_i^{(\ell)}$ itself co-evolves with the state. Layer-wise sharpening of $\Delta_i^{(\ell)}$ is a model-side hypothesis verified empirically by Figure~\ref{fig:attention_mass}. A full block adds residual connections, normalization, and an FFN; under a common local Lipschitz constant on a ball containing both perturbed and unperturbed trajectories \citep{xiong2020layer, kim2021lipschitz}, Appendix~\ref{app:ffn_lipschitz} shows that the per-block perturbation amplitude scales linearly with $w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}$, so iterating across finite depth yields a finite cumulative bound. Combined with Eq.~\eqref{eq:decay_main}, the perturbation enters strongly at early blocks and shrinks as evidence accumulates, without requiring persistence throughout the chain.

\subsection{Output Support Expansion Beyond Temperature}\label{sec:output_shift}
% ================================================================

Temperature sampling is rank-preserving: $p_i^{(\tau)}\propto p_i^{1/\tau}$ implies $p_i>p_j\iff p_i^{(\tau)}>p_j^{(\tau)}$ for any $\tau>0$, so it cannot change token rankings at a fixed decoding state. RSP, by acting before the output softmax, can. To isolate the conditions under which it does, we work with the local affine surrogate $z_i^{\mathrm{lin}}(\bar h):= z_i + a_i^\top\bar h$, $a_i := \nabla_{\bar h} z_i(0)$, valid in a neighborhood of $\bar h=0$ (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). Throughout this subsection $\bar h$ is treated as a per-token surrogate; the multi-token compounding through softmax-weighted attention is reported empirically in Section~\ref{sec:diversity}.

\begin{proposition}[Stochastic ranking]\label{prop:stochastic_ranking}
If $a_i\neq a_j$ for some token pair $(i,j)$ and the centered prompt law assigns positive probability to every nonempty open subset of $\mathbb{R}^d$, then $0<\Pr_{\bar h}(z_i^{\mathrm{lin}}(\bar h)>z_j^{\mathrm{lin}}(\bar h))<1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Top-$K$ entry]\label{prop:support_expansion}
Let $J_K(s_t)$ be the baseline top-$K$ token set at decoding state $s_t$ and let $i\notin J_K(s_t)$. Define $\mathcal{G}_{i,K} := \{\bar h\in\mathbb{R}^d:\#\{j\neq i: z_j^{\mathrm{lin}}(\bar h)>z_i^{\mathrm{lin}}(\bar h)\}\le K-1\}$. A sufficient condition for $\mathcal{G}_{i,K}$ to contain a nonempty open set is the existence of $\bar h_0$ at which token $i$ outranks all but at most $K-1$ other tokens with a strict margin; the affine surrogate is continuous, so an open ball around $\bar h_0$ lies in $\mathcal{G}_{i,K}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). If this condition holds and the centered prompt law has full open support, then $\Pr_{\bar h}(\bar h\in\mathcal{G}_{i,K})>0$.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:support_expansion} is intentionally conditional: it makes no claim for tokens dominated by the baseline top-$K$ in the surrogate. Temperature scaling reaches no $\mathcal{G}_{i,K}$ at all, because it never changes rank; the surrogate-level mechanism here is branch opening, not probability flattening.

% ================================================================
\subsection{From Independent Perturbations to Pass@$N$}\label{sec:multipath}
% ================================================================

Set $\mu_i := \Pr_{\bar h}(\bar h\in\mathcal{G}_{i,K}) > 0$. Across $N$ independent fresh RSP draws, the probability that at least one admits $i$ into top-$K$ is $1-(1-\mu_i)^N \ge 1-\exp(-N\mu_i)$, so independent resampling converts a small per-draw branch-opening probability into an exponentially saturating coverage rate. Lifting this to task accuracy requires one task-side hypothesis on whether opened branches are productive.

\begin{proposition}[Exploration meets multi-path]\label{prop:multi_path}
Fix a problem $x$. Run the deterministic baseline once and draw $N$ independent fresh RSPs, decoding once per sample. Let $\mathcal{T}^\star(x)$ be the nonempty set of correct trajectories and $p_{\mathrm{base}}(x)\in\{0,1\}$ the baseline correctness indicator. Assume \emph{(M1)} for each $n$, the marginal probability that the $n$-th RSP run produces a trajectory in $\mathcal{T}^\star(x)$ is at least $p_{\min}(x)>0$, and \emph{(M2)} the $N$ RSP runs are mutually independent. Then $\Pr\!\big(\mathrm{Pass@}(N{+}1)\text{ correct on }x\big) \ge \max\!\big\{p_{\mathrm{base}}(x),\,1-(1-p_{\min}(x))^N\big\} \ge 1-e^{-Np_{\min}(x)}$.
\end{proposition}

Assumption (M1) is task-side and not derivable from the mechanism alone. Section~\ref{sec:diversity} reports its empirical signature: RSP-induced trajectories include both improvements and regressions, with net gain accumulating under repeated sampling exactly as the proposition predicts.
