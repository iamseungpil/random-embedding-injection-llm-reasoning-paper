\section{Random Soft Prompts and Why They Work}\label{sec:why_rsp_works}

This section unfolds RSP's mechanism along \emph{a single quantity}. RSP does not add to the hidden state directly but enters only through attention weights, so within a rollout (one full autoregressive decode under one sampled RSP) its instantaneous influence collects into a single scalar --- the \emph{attention mass} $w$ assigned to RSP tokens. Early in decoding this quantity is large enough to branch the hidden state into multiple directions (\S\ref{sec:improve}); as the autoregressive cache grows, the upper bound on the \emph{same scalar} automatically narrows and the response converges toward the goal (\S\ref{sec:converge}). The role of randomness is not to imitate the embedding distribution but to supply \emph{independent} perturbations across rollouts so that, without any prior weighting on a worst-case direction, each rollout opens a fresh branch. We keep the main statements and intuition here; further proofs and derivations are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

% ================================================================
\subsection{Random Soft Prompt}\label{sec:rsp_definition}
% ================================================================

We start with the object being analyzed. Let $\mathbf{W}_E \in \mathbb{R}^{V\times d}$ denote the pretrained token-embedding matrix, with $V$ the vocabulary size and $d$ the hidden dimension. Write its entrywise mean and standard deviation as $\mu_E := \tfrac{1}{Vd}\sum_{i,k}[\mathbf{W}_E]_{i,k}$ and $\sigma_E := \mathrm{std}(\mathbf{W}_E)$. An RSP of length $L$ is a sequence of continuous vectors $\mathbf{H} = [h_1,\dots,h_L]\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times d}$ drawn independently from
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rsp_def}
h_j \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}(\mu_E\mathbf{1}_d,\;\sigma_E^2\mathbf{I}_d), \quad j=1,\dots,L.
\end{equation}
The centered form $\bar h_j := h_j - \mu_E\mathbf{1}_d \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_E^2\mathbf{I}_d)$ is a zero-mean isotropic Gaussian. The main text uses the \emph{suffix} form $[\mathbf{X};\mathbf{H}]$ --- appending the RSP after the input embedding sequence $\mathbf{X}$ --- as the default; other positions (prefix, infix \citep{kang2025model, xu2025softcot, ye2025thinking, zhang2025memgen}) are reported as ablations in \S\ref{sec:performance} and Appendix~\ref{app:ablation}. RSP is \emph{not a learnable parameter} --- it receives no gradient and is freshly drawn for each rollout. For repeated-sampling protocols, prompt draws and decoding randomness are kept independent across compared rollouts.

% ================================================================
\subsection{Exploration: RSP strengthens early-stage exploration}\label{sec:improve}\label{sec:multipath}
% ================================================================

Inserting a random prompt concentrates attention on it, and that concentration is what amplifies exploration. The effect of a single RSP injection on the hidden state can be examined along two questions --- \emph{how much} and \emph{in which direction}. The former is decided by a single scalar $w$ inside one attention head; the latter, by the distribution of $\mathbf{H}$. RSP's design choice --- an isotropic Gaussian --- addresses both at once.

Consider one attention head in self-attention layer $\ell$ that attends $n\ge1$ unmasked real tokens together with $L\ge1$ RSP tokens, with all attention logits finite. Write the attention weights as $\alpha_{ij}^{(\ell)}$ and the value vectors on the real and RSP sides as $\mathbf{v}_j^{(\ell)},\mathbf{v}_{r_j}^{(\ell)}$. Defining the total attention mass on random tokens $w_{r,i}^{(\ell)} := \sum_{j=1}^L \alpha_{i,n+j}^{(\ell)}$, the real-token mass is positive and the head output decomposes \emph{exactly} as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decomp}
\mathbf{o}_i^{(\ell)}
\;=\;
(1-w_{r,i}^{(\ell)})\,\tilde{\mathbf{o}}_i^{(\ell)}
\;+\;
\boldsymbol{\eta}_i^{(\ell)},
\quad
\tilde{\mathbf{o}}_i^{(\ell)} := \sum_{j=1}^n \tfrac{\alpha_{ij}^{(\ell)}}{1-w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}}\,\mathbf{v}_j^{(\ell)},
\quad
\boldsymbol{\eta}_i^{(\ell)} := \sum_{j=1}^L \alpha_{i,n+j}^{(\ell)}\mathbf{v}_{r_j}^{(\ell)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proof}[Derivation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomp}]
Write the head output as $\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{ij}^{(\ell)}\mathbf{v}_{j}^{(\ell)}+\sum_{j=1}^{L}\alpha_{i,n+j}^{(\ell)}\mathbf{v}_{r_j}^{(\ell)}$, then factor $1-w_{r,i}^{(\ell)} = \sum_{j=1}^n \alpha_{ij}^{(\ell)}>0$ out of the real-token sum. No approximation beyond the softmax-normalization identity is used.
\end{proof}

This single quantity $w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}$ controls both the attenuation ratio $1-w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}$ on the real signal and the upper bound on the random contribution $\|\boldsymbol{\eta}_i^{(\ell)}\| \le w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}\,\max_j \|\mathbf{v}_{r_j}^{(\ell)}\|$. The \emph{magnitude} of RSP-induced variation thus reduces to one number in $[0,1]$. Early in decoding the cache is short and this value is non-negligible, so a single RSP draw can perturb next-token decisions.

The scalar $w$ alone, however, controls only \emph{how much} and not \emph{where} the perturbation lands; the direction is decided by the distribution of $\mathbf{H}$. A deterministic token sequence pins $\boldsymbol{\eta}$ to a single direction, leaving its orthogonal complement unperturbed; uniform sampling from the vocabulary is random but inherits the anisotropic geometry of the token-embedding table. Within a fixed variance budget, the \emph{covariance} that maximizes the worst-case directional variance is uniquely isotropic (Proposition below).

\begin{proposition}[Direction-agnostic minimax coverage]\label{thm:minimax_directional_coverage}
Let $D$ be any zero-mean distribution on $\mathbb{R}^d$ with covariance $\Sigma_D$ and trace budget $\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma_D)\le\rho^2$. Then $\min_{\|b\|=1}\mathrm{Var}_{h\sim D}(b^\top h) = \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_D) \le \rho^2/d$, with equality if and only if $\Sigma_D = (\rho^2/d)\mathbf{I}_d$.
\end{proposition}

The Gaussian RSP exactly satisfies this equality condition with $\Sigma = \sigma_E^2 \mathbf{I}_d$ ($\rho^2 = d\sigma_E^2$), and the Gaussian form additionally provides \emph{full support} on $\mathbb{R}^d$ (Appendix~\ref{app:why_random}). The two properties together produce single-shot branching: under a local affine assumption, the $\bar h$ region that drops the baseline top-1 token and elevates another into the top-$K$ is an \emph{open} region, and the full-support Gaussian assigns it positive probability whenever the region is non-empty. The monotone temperature transform preserves token rankings and therefore cannot reach the same region --- RSP's \emph{rank-changing} property follows from this (Appendix~\ref{app:topk_entry}). Together, these properties diversify the response into multiple branches early in reasoning.

\subsection{Annealing: KV cache growth dilutes RSP attention}\label{sec:converge}
% ================================================================

The early branching that RSP introduces stabilizes naturally as decoding proceeds: the upper bound on the same scalar $w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}$ defined in \S\ref{sec:improve} narrows along the sequence axis with autoregressive cache growth. Each step appends one real-token term while the number of RSP terms is fixed; freezing the logit gap between real and RSP tokens, this asymmetry yields the following exact bound.

\begin{theorem}[Attention-mass decay under cache growth]\label{thm:decay}
For a query attending $n\ge1$ real tokens and $L\ge1$ RSP tokens with finite logits, let $\Delta_i^{(\ell)} := \min_{j\in[n]}\mathbf{q}_i^\top\mathbf{k}_j - \max_{j'\in[L]}\mathbf{q}_i^\top\mathbf{k}_{r_{j'}}$ denote the pre-scale logit gap between real and random tokens. In scaled dot-product attention,
\[
w_{r,i}^{(\ell)} \;\le\; \frac{L}{L + n\,\exp(\Delta_i^{(\ell)}/\sqrt d)},
\]
and the right-hand side is strictly decreasing in $n$ and tends to $0$ at fixed $L$ and $\Delta_i^{(\ell)}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $s_j := \mathbf{q}_i^\top\mathbf{k}_j/\sqrt d$, $s_{r_{j'}} := \mathbf{q}_i^\top\mathbf{k}_{r_{j'}}/\sqrt d$, $s_{r,\max} := \max_{j'} s_{r_{j'}}$, and $R := \sum_{j'=1}^L \exp(s_{r_{j'}})$. The gap definition gives $s_j \ge \Delta_i^{(\ell)}/\sqrt d + s_{r,\max}$ for every real $j$, and $\exp(s_{r,\max}) \ge R/L$ holds because the max dominates the average; combining the two and summing over the $n$ real keys yields $\sum_{j=1}^n \exp(s_j) \ge (n/L)\exp(\Delta_i^{(\ell)}/\sqrt d)\,R$. Substituting into $w_{r,i}^{(\ell)} = R/(\sum_j\exp(s_j) + R)$ gives
\[
w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}
\;\le\;
\frac{R}{(n/L)\exp(\Delta_i^{(\ell)}/\sqrt d)\,R + R}
\;=\;
\frac{L}{L + n\,\exp(\Delta_i^{(\ell)}/\sqrt d)}.
\]
The derivative of the right-hand side in $n$ is $-L\exp(\Delta_i^{(\ell)}/\sqrt d)/(L + n\exp(\Delta_i^{(\ell)}/\sqrt d))^2 < 0$, so the bound is strictly decreasing and tends to $0$ as $n\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

Two mechanisms are encoded in this single line. The RSP length $L$ sits in the numerator and sets the \emph{maximum} influence; the real-token count $n$ enters the denominator and grows automatically every step, so the \emph{upper bound} on the influence narrows under the same $L$. The theorem guarantees only the sequence-direction attenuation of the upper envelope at fixed (layer, query position, logit gap), not a layer-direction monotone decay; queries, keys, and hidden states co-evolve, so $\Delta_i^{(\ell)}$ also moves and the realized $w_{r,i}^{(\ell)}$ need not decrease at every step. The accurate reading is therefore not ``realized mass decreases monotonically per step'' but ``the attainable upper bound at a fixed gap decreases monotonically.'' Because Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomp} ties the random contribution to the same scalar, the perturbation magnitude inherits the same envelope, giving RSP an implicit explore-then-exploit reading: it opens widely early in generation and closes naturally as the cache accumulates. Lifting this single-shot branching to task accuracy via independent re-sampling across rollouts (\S\ref{sec:rsp_definition}) requires two assumptions --- (M1) one execution reaches a correct trajectory with marginal probability $p_{\min}(x)>0$, and (M2) the $N$ executions are mutually independent. Under both, a standard ensemble argument yields $\Pr(\mathrm{Pass@}N \text{ on } x) \ge 1 - (1-p_{\min}(x))^N$ (Appendix~\ref{app:passN_ensemble}). Sharing one RSP across all samples breaks (M2) and removes the accumulation, so \emph{independence} is the key --- \S\ref{sec:experimental_analysis} verifies this signature empirically.
