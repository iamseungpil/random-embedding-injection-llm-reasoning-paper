\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{Summary.}
We studied Random Soft Prompts (RSPs), continuous vectors sampled from an isotropic Gaussian fitted to the entrywise mean and variance of the pretrained embedding table and injected without training. Empirically, RSPs raise early-stage token entropy and varentropy while later generation stabilizes (Section~\ref{sec:experimental_analysis}); theoretically, RSPs induce stochastic token rankings and admit baseline-excluded tokens into the effective top-$K$ candidate set, an effect temperature scaling cannot reproduce (Section~\ref{sec:why_rsp_works}); together, RSPs combined with temperature sampling produce more diverse reasoning trajectories under Pass@$n$.

\paragraph{Implications, limitations, and future work.}
RSP is rank-changing and therefore complementary to rank-preserving temperature sampling, suggesting a sampling-level remedy for entropy collapse beyond DAPO \citep{yu2025dapo} and CE-GPPO \citep{su2025cegppo} in GRPO \citep{shao2024deepseekmath}; training-time validation against these baselines is the natural next step. Our experiments cover Qwen2.5-Math and LLaMA-3.1 on MATH-500, GSM8K, and AIME24; the output-shift propositions are stated for a per-token affine surrogate (Proposition~\ref{prop:multi_path} additionally requires a task-side $p_{\min}(x){>}0$), and a principled choice of RSP length and injection position remains open.
