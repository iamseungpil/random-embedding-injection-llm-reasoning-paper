\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied Random Soft Prompts (RSPs), training-free isotropic Gaussian vectors fitted to pretrained embedding statistics. Latent-prompt methods share the same entropy signature, the gain vanishes when one RSP is shared across samples (\S\ref{sec:experimental_analysis}), DAPO + RSP yields a modest late-training advantage (\S\ref{sec:application}), and the mechanism reduces to a single attention-mass scalar whose fixed-gap upper bound shrinks under KV cache growth (\S\ref{sec:why_rsp_works}). This signature emerges from purely random input-side perturbation, suggesting part of what trained soft prompts deliver is a \emph{structural by-product of injection}; RSP composes orthogonally with sampling-based pipelines as a near-zero-cost diversity expander. Open questions: generalization beyond RoPE decoders and math, deep-layer sharpening outside Theorem~\ref{thm:decay}'s envelope, multi-seed DAPO variance, held-out hyperparameter selection (\S\ref{sec:setup}).
