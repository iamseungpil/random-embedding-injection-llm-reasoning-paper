Layer-wise Energy Landscape Mechanism of Random Soft Prompts (RSP)

CRITICAL CONTEXT (read first):
- This figure is about LANGUAGE MODEL REASONING, not adversarial robustness, not optimization training.
- The "energy" is the Modern Hopfield energy associated with each transformer LAYER's attention.
- The "trajectory" is the hidden-state evolution across transformer LAYERS during a SINGLE forward pass.
- DO NOT depict adversarial attacks. DO NOT depict gradient descent on a loss surface. DO NOT depict embedding scatter plots.
- The figure must show TWO TRAJECTORIES on the SAME LAYER-INDEXED ENERGY LANDSCAPE: one without RSP (gets stuck), one with RSP (escapes via random kicks that automatically anneal).

CORE MESSAGE:
Without RSP, the transformer's deterministic layer-by-layer descent traps the hidden state in the basin of its initial state — a shallow local minimum corresponding to a suboptimal reasoning branch. With RSP, each layer adds a small stochastic kick whose magnitude decays as the model accumulates real-key context. Because the kick is isotropic (random in direction), it has positive probability of crossing any energy barrier, allowing the trajectory to reach deeper basins (better reasoning branches). The decay schedule prevents over-perturbation in late layers where commitment is desired.

CAUSAL STORY THE FIGURE TELLS:
"At each transformer layer, attention performs a Hopfield energy descent. Without perturbation, the descent is fully deterministic and gets trapped in whatever basin the initial state belongs to. RSP injects an isotropic random perturbation η at each layer, decomposed via the same scalar w_r that also controls its dilution. Because η is random in direction, it has positive probability of crossing any barrier — including barriers separating shallow and deep basins. Because w_r decays as real-key context grows, the perturbation magnitude automatically shrinks toward late layers/positions, mimicking simulated annealing without an external schedule."
