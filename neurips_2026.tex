\begin{abstract}
Even untrained random embeddings, when injected into an LLM input, change the model's reasoning behavior in non-trivial ways. Recent \textit{soft prompt} methods optimize trainable virtual-token embeddings to improve mathematical reasoning, but it is unclear whether the gain comes from the \emph{learning} or from the \emph{injection} itself. To separate the two, we remove training entirely: a Random Soft Prompt (RSP) is a continuous vector sampled from an isotropic Gaussian fitted to the entrywise mean and variance of the pretrained embedding table, and injected as is. Our analysis has two parts. Within one rollout, an exact attention decomposition shows how an RSP can perturb early next-token decisions and then fade as the autoregressive cache grows. Across rollouts, isotropic prompts are direction-agnostic under a variance budget and assign positive probability to every nonempty open set, so independent resampling can reopen early branches; under a separate task-side success assumption, this yields Pass@$N$ growth. The experiments match these claims: zero-training RSP remains competitive with optimized soft prompts, early-token distributions diversify and later stabilize, and Pass@$N$ gains disappear when the same RSP is shared across samples. We close by discussing the implications of this diversity for GRPO training, where trajectory diversity is critical for learning signal quality.
\end{abstract}
