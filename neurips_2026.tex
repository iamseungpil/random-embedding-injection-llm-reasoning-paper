\begin{abstract}
Even untrained random embeddings, when injected into an LLM input, change the model's reasoning behavior in non-trivial ways. Recent \textit{soft prompt} methods optimize trainable virtual-token embeddings to improve mathematical reasoning, but it is unclear whether the gain comes from the \emph{learning} or from the \emph{injection} itself. To separate the two, we strip the training step entirely: a Random Soft Prompt (RSP) is a continuous vector sampled from an isotropic Gaussian fitted to the entrywise mean and variance of the pretrained embedding table, and injected as is. Two simple observations carry the analysis. First, the influence of an RSP is largest in the early generation steps and naturally fades as more tokens are appended---each layer's attention assigns less weight to the random tokens once the autoregressive cache has filled in. Second, a single drawn RSP is fixed within one rollout and selects one branch different from the baseline; because each rollout draws a fresh RSP, branches from different rollouts accumulate, and a token rarely seen by the baseline can enter the candidate set in some rollouts. We make both observations quantitative inside the transformer, and verify them with three experiments: baseline-accuracy recovery, an early-token diversification followed by late-stage stabilization, and Pass@$N$ saturation that disappears once the same RSP is shared across samples. We close by discussing the implications of this diversity for GRPO training, where trajectory diversity is critical for learning signal quality.
\end{abstract}
